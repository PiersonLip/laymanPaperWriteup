In this paper we presented a number of measured properties from in-depth simulations of [[Isolated Black Hole|isolated]] [[black hole|black holes]] (BHs).

These BHs are especially relevant currently, as both [[Roman Space Telescope]] and [[Gaia DR4]] are very likely to detect/reveal a large quantity of ones previously undiscovered. The location of these black holes will of course discovered by these telescopes, but as equally important is the lives which they've lived. Answering what (and where!) were they before becoming black holes tells us a massive amount about the physics surrounding.

In this paper we present the [[evolutionary channels|channels]] from which black holes in our milky way (MW) form, finding that the vast majority of black holes in our MW are isolated, and largely formed through [[mergers]] and [[disruptions]].

Among the systems which are *not* [[disrupted]], we find detached BH-BH systems are the largest contributor, and at lower masses we find that these systems are predominantly experiencing [[stable mass transfer]] before BH formation.

Finally, in order to glean a better idea of observational properties, we embed this synthetic populations into a model of the milky-way, and evolve their kinematics and orbits[^2], as well as doing [[microlensing]] calculations to help predict the observeabilitliy of them in the context of the Gaia mission.
From this, we find a unique population which is not present in other simulation methods.
